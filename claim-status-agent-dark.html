Claim Status Agent

An AI agent that checks claim status with insurance payers automatically and turns messy, payer-specific responses into one clear answer billing teams can act on.

Role
Product owner
Domain
Healthcare revenue cycle
Channels
APIs, clearinghouse, RPA, voice

Live, scaling
TL;DR

I moved claim status from payer-by-payer logic to one universal way of reading payer responses, defined what “done” really means for a status check, and used failure analysis to fix the real bottlenecks — most of which sat upstream of the bot. Since April 2024 it has automated 2.1M+ status checks, saving about 140,000 hours of manual work.

Problem

Checking a claim is slow, manual and inconsistent

After a claim is submitted, someone has to find out what happened to it: paid, denied, still processing, or never received. For billing teams this means logging into payer portals, waiting on hold, and copying answers into the billing system — claim by claim.

Automation existed, but each payer returned status differently and the logic to interpret it was written payer by payer. Every new payer meant new code, and nobody could say with confidence how much of the work was truly done without a human.

Users

Who it serves

  • AR specialists and ops teams who work claim follow-up queues every day.
  • Provider clients who need predictable cash flow and fewer write-offs.
  • Leadership who need an honest view of what agent actually completes.
Approach

What I changed

  1. 01One way to read every payerI pushed for a universal, amount-based derivation — “the money tells the story” — instead of payer-specific code. Every response maps to five statuses: Paid, Denied, In process, Claim not on file, Needs review.
  2. 02Define “done” before measuring itI introduced a completeness check at charge level, so a status only counts as complete when the fields billing teams need are actually there.
  3. 03More ways to reach payersClearinghouse and payer APIs first, RPA where needed, and voice AI as a fallback for payers that can only be reached by phone.
  4. 04Prioritise with dataI ranked payer upgrades with RICE scoring across months of transaction data, so engineering time went to the biggest unautomated volumes first.
Insight

Most failures weren’t the bot’s fault

Detailed failure analyses with clients showed the dominant causes were upstream: wrong member IDs, wrong payer assignments and missing data in the billing system. Fixing those with clients moved results more than any bot change.

The fastest way to raise automation success was to fix the data going into it — not to write more automation.

Next

Where it goes from here

Now building: the Notes Agent. Today a status check ends in our system, and someone still copies the result into the client’s practice management system (PMS) — the software their billing team works in. The Notes Agent writes the latest claim status straight into the PMS as a claim note, so the update is waiting where people already work.

After that comes routing on the derived status: for a denied claim, read the denial reason and hand it to the right next step — for example, an eligibility agent — or suggest the best next action to the ops user.
